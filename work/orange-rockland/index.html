Energy & Utilities • Product Design • 2025 - 2026

O&R CARE (Customer Analytics, Reporting and Engagement) is an initiative that includes a residential dashboard for exploring energy use and personalized recommendations.

I led discovery through handoff as Senior Product Designer, aligning O&R product and program teams with technical partners. The MVP launched mid-2026.

Challenge

A list of programs wasn't enough

Customers needed to understand which energy options could fit their home, what those options involved, and where to begin before committing to a program.

Interest didn’t make the next step obvious

O&R wanted to grow participation in energy efficiency and clean energy programs. For customers, the path from a high energy bill to a home improvement could involve unfamiliar technology, program rules, assessments, and contractors.

The opportunity was to connect those pieces: help customers understand their options and find a useful next step, whether they were ready to pursue a program or still learning.

Designing alongside a changing product

Program offerings and data integrations were evolving as the dashboard took shape. I used journey maps, wireframes, and design reviews to make the experience tangible and align product, business, and technical partners around an MVP.

Research

Customers wanted a trusted guide

Research revealed gaps in energy knowledge and concerns about cost, disruption, and contractors. Prototype feedback reinforced the need for professional validation alongside self-service information.

What we learned

Hard to get started

The process and next steps felt overwhelming.

Value is uncertain

Costs felt risky and savings were hard to predict.

Trust is essential

Customers wanted credible guidance and contractors.

Design implication

Help customers understand their options and find support before committing to a project.

The barriers started before enrollment

West Monroe’s weatherization research included seven small focus groups with prospective participants and four interviews with customers who had completed projects. Participants described uncertainty about cost, disruption, potential savings, and where to find a contractor. Many were also unfamiliar with available programs and rebates.

Separate trust research with O&R customers found that limited understanding of energy use and unclear information could make it harder to trust the utility’s guidance. These qualitative findings helped frame education and clarity as part of the product experience.

Make room for human guidance

In pre-launch prototype research with O&R customers, feedback reinforced the need for professional validation alongside self-service information. I incorporated a “Speak with an advisor” option so customers could ask questions before deciding what to do next.

Design

From information to supported action

I designed an experience that pairs energy information and recommendations with lower-commitment next steps, including an assessment and the option to speak with an advisor.

Start with the customer’s energy context

The dashboard brought energy usage and comparisons together with recommendations and resources. I designed the experience to give customers a starting point for exploring their home’s energy needs, rather than asking them to navigate a list of programs alone.

Offer a next step for different levels of readiness

Recommendation cards paired education about improvements with paths to programs and resources. Alongside those links, a virtual assessment and advisor support gave customers ways to learn more before committing to a larger project.

Build a foundation for learning

The MVP launched with usage information, assessment access, recommendations, and program resources. Two-way Brillion data exchange and behavioral analytics came later, creating opportunities to better understand how customers move through the experience.

Next opportunity: guidance beyond the click

Program link clicks show exploration, but they don’t tell us what happened after customers left the dashboard. A potential next step is a project tracker that shows milestones, status, and the next action—from assessment through completion. It would require reliable cross-system status data before it could be validated or measured.
